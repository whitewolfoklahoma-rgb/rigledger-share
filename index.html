How RigLedger works

The idea

One file, no account, no subscription, no per-truck monthly meter — your books never leave this device. Log loads, fuel stops, and miles by state as you run; RigLedger keeps your true cost per mile, your break-even rate, who's paid and who hasn't, your quarterly IFTA worksheet, and your estimated taxes current.

Income: cash basis

A load counts as income when the money is in hand: status Paid or Factored. Factoring fees you pay go in Expenses (Factoring fees). Pending loads are receivables — they show in aging, not in profit. If your business files taxes on the accrual method, tell your preparer this app's timing.

Cost per mile & break-even

Total cost = all fuel (Fuel tab) + all expenses (Expenses tab — fuel is never entered there, so nothing double-counts). Cost per mile = total cost ÷ total miles, where total miles = loaded + deadhead across all loads in the year. Your break-even rate is the same number: a load paid below it loses money at current costs. Fixed CPM = fixed costs ÷ miles (insurance, lease, permits, phone — due whether you roll or not); variable CPM = (fuel + variable expenses) ÷ miles. Per-load profit = load pay − (that load's total miles × the year's CPM). It's an allocation estimate — the year's average cost spread over the load's miles — not a trip-level accounting. Deadhead miles are fully counted: they burn fuel and tires exactly like loaded ones.

IFTA worksheet

Per quarter: fleet MPG = total miles in all jurisdictions (your miles-by-state entries) ÷ total gallons purchased in all jurisdictions (your Fuel log). For each jurisdiction: taxable gallons = jurisdiction miles ÷ fleet MPG; net taxable gallons = taxable gallons − gallons purchased in that jurisdiction; net tax = net taxable gallons × the rate you entered for that jurisdiction and quarter. Each jurisdiction's net is rounded to cents, then summed for the quarter total. A positive total is tax due; negative is a credit. IFTA tax rates are set by each jurisdiction and change quarterly — that's why the rate table ships empty: enter the quarter's official rates (your base state's IFTA return packet or the IFTA rate matrix) before filing. Rates are stored per quarter, so last quarter's rates never leak into this quarter's return. This worksheet prepares your numbers; you still file the official return with your base jurisdiction. Not tax advice.

Estimated taxes

Net profit = paid/factored load revenue − fuel − expenses. Self-employment tax = profit × 92.35% × 15.3%. Half of that is deducted before the federal income-tax estimate: federal = (profit − half SE) × your federal effective-rate %. State = profit × your state %. Quarterly suggestions annualize your profit through each quarter's end, the standard safe-harbor style planning approach.

What's NOT in these estimates

Planning worksheets, not a tax return and not tax advice. Left out: the Social Security wage-base cap, the standard deduction and real tax brackets (that's why you supply an effective rate), per-diem deductions, depreciation of the truck, other income, W-2 wages, and credits. A tax professional who knows trucking will beat these worksheets — use them to avoid surprises and underpayment penalties, not as your final number.
